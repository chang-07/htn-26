Four ways the plan card could look

The PNG is the only surface you control — the caption rows underneath are Apple's chrome, in Apple's font, with an app icon you can't set. So the image carries the whole idea. Each direction is shown in the two states that matter, because the card redraws in the same bubble as the plan progresses.

What you can and can't set

image_url
Yours. Renders for everyone, app installed or not. This is the design.
image_title / _subtitle
Apple overlays these on top of your image. Every direction below leaves them unset and bakes type into the PNG instead — otherwise they collide.
caption / subcaption
Bottom-left, Apple's font. Not stylable.
trailing_caption / _subcaption
Bottom-right, same deal.
app icon
Fixed to your extension's icon. Not settable per message.
01

Ticket stub

Treats the plan as a physical thing you're holding. The tear-off stub gives the booked state somewhere obvious to land, and the perforation reads instantly at bubble size. Warmest of the four, and the one that feels least like software. Risk: the stub steals ~20% of the width, so long venue names get tight.

02

Departure board

Built for the thing the card actually does well: changing in place. Live vote counts tick up the right-hand column with no message spam, and the amber-on-black holds up at thumbnail size better than anything else here. Reads as infrastructure, in a good way. Risk: the coldest of the four — a hangout with friends shouldn't necessarily feel like a train station.

03

Invitation poster

The only direction that leads with a voice instead of data. Big serif, one saturated ground, options demoted to chips — it looks like something a person made, which is the whole premise of an agent that hangs out in your group chat. Also the most screenshot-able. Risk: it holds the least information, and the colour has to change per state to carry meaning.

04

Itemized receipt

Leans into the agent doing errands on your behalf — every line is something it actually did, so the card doubles as a log. Scales to the parts of the plan beyond the reservation (ride, food, tickets) better than the other three, which all assume one venue. Risk: dense mono at bubble size is the hardest to read on a phone held at arm's length.

Two things to know before you pick. These are HTML mockups, but the real PNG renders through Satori (workers-og), which is flexbox only — no CSS grid, no transform: rotate, no background-image gradients on text. Directions 01–04 are all reachable, but the receipt's rotated BOOKED stamp and the ticket's dotted perforation each need a workaround. Second: image_url requires a trusted chat with inbound activity — the image won't render into a cold thread until someone has texted the number first.
